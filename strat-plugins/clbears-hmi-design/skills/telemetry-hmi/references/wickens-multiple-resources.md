# Wickens (2008): Multiple resources and mental workload
*Human Factors 50(3), 449-455. Paraphrased.*

**Model.** Attention isn't one pool. Tasks interfere less when they use different resources on four dimensions:
- **Stage:** perception/cognition vs response selection/execution.
- **Code:** spatial vs verbal (in perception, memory and response: graphs vs digits, manual vs voice).
- **Modality:** visual vs auditory (perception only).
- **Visual channel:** focal (reading, symbols) vs ambient (motion, orientation, peripheral).

Predicted interference = demand of each task + how much they share resources. The model predicts
*relative* differences between designs, not absolute "acceptable" workload. Workload matters most when
total demand exceeds capacity (the "red line"); below it there is spare capacity for the unexpected.

**Allocation is imperfect.** Interruptions, cognitive tunnelling and auditory pre-emption pull attention
away from the "right" task.

**Implications for UBC Solar displays:**
- Pit crew and strategy listen to the radio (auditory-verbal) while scanning screens. Favour **spatial/analog**
  encodings (bands, trends, positions) over reading many digits, which competes with verbal processing.
- Keep text short and stable; let motion and colour change (ambient/peripheral) flag abnormal states.
- Keep displays under the red line: fewer, better panels at the top; detail lower or collapsed.
- For the driver (DRD LCD): the primary task is visual-manual driving, so glances must be short and
  information pre-digested.
